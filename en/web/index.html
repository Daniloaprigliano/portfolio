Websites and applications

I design and develop web applications end to end, and I look after the sites of associations, magazines and cultural projects: content architecture, domain, maintenance, security. For an archive or an association a website is a working tool, not a shop window.

A dashboard that holds archives, calendar and research together

In progress

web application, continuous development · not public

An application I designed and wrote entirely myself: it runs as a worker on serverlessinfrastructure, it has no database of its own and at every request it queries and cross-references the sources (an archive of notes and projects, mail, the calendar, a library, a press review with a voice reader), returning data computed on the fly. It includes an authenticated API through which automated agents read and write. It has no public address because it holds my own mail and archives: I mention it because it is the proof of what I can build on my own, and whoever teaches these tools should also have assembled them.

«il ribelle», digital archive of the clandestine paper

2013

digitisation, indexing and publication · il-ribelle.it

The first archive I built: the complete run of the Fiamme Verdi clandestine paper, digitised issue by issue and made searchable. The problem was editorial rather than technical – giving someone looking for a name, a date or a fact a way to reach it without already knowing where to look. The story of the project is on the public history page.

Fiamme Verdi of Brescia

Since 2014

website and digital history, continuous maintenance · fiammeverdibrescia.it

Documents, biographies and a chronology of the partisan war in Valtellina and on the Mortirolo. I have kept it running since 2014 – domain, updates, security – and it was this work on the sources that produced the Marsilio chapter.

The Fifteen Martyrs of Piazzale Loreto

Ongoing

site and podcast, built with a final-year class · i15martiri.daniloaprigliano.it

An archive site made together with students: they find the documents, write the entries and record the voices; I build the structure and teach the method. It is where web work and classroom work become the same thing. The classroom project is described elsewhere.

The Feltrinelli Foundation’s digital exhibitions

2019

digital staging of five exhibitions · risorsedigitali.fondazionefeltrinelli.it

Five exhibitions built for the web rather than carried onto it: «Il Che vive!», «La Polonia di Solidarność», «Una storia europea chiamata Rivoluzione», «La nuova frontiera americana», «Cile 1973». Each has its own reading structure, because a room and a screen are not visited in the same way.

«Mai più Piazza Fontana»

2024

site of the schools project of the «Emilio Caldara» study centre · daniloaprigliano.github.io/maipiupiazzafontana

Designed and written for the study day I organised at the Civico Polo «A. Manzoni»: programme, speakers, materials and documents stay online after the event, which is the only serious reason to build a site for a study day at all.

School of European Citizenship

Ongoing

digital editing, with the Feltrinelli Foundation · scuoladicittadinanzaeuropea.it

The training programme for schools on European institutions and sources: I look after the digital side and am credited among the authors. Teaching how to read a European document is, quite literally, teaching how to read a source.

Teaching materials, built by hand

2024–2025

classroom prototypes · the Risorgimento timeline · Benjamin

Two tools written for my own classes and left public: a navigable chronology of the Risorgimento and a prototype for working on a difficult text. They are here because they show what it means to build your own material instead of adapting someone else’s.

Authors’ sites and magazines

Ongoing

design, development and maintenance · carolinacavalli.it

Personal sites for writers, and the cultural magazine Galápagos, which I founded. I follow the whole cycle – content architecture, development, domain and hosting, updates and security – on the principle that for an archive or an association a website is a working tool, not a shop window. Whoever commissions a site from me does not then have to find someone else to keep it alive.

What is visible here is the technical side of each of these projects: how it is made and how it is kept alive. The content – the stories, the documents, the classes – is on the public history and civic work pages. This site is written by hand in HTML and CSS and published on GitHub Pages; the code is public.
